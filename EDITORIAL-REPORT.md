# Editorial report

Scope: every page except the six approved designs (homepage, Password Security, Backup & Recovery, Business Wi-Fi, SIM Swap Fraud, WhatsApp Hijacking), which were left as approved.

## What was checked

- Every page was scanned for stock phrases and machine-sounding patterns ("in today's digital age", "landscape", "robust", "seamless", "leverage", "unlock", "empower", "holistic", "navigate", "ultimately", "moreover" and similar), for em and en dashes, and for American spellings.
- Result: no stock phrases and no American spellings were found. The only issue was em dashes.
- This was a keyword and punctuation check, not an AI-detector. Detectors are unreliable, so a person should still read the key pages before launch.

## What was changed

- **Em dashes removed: 77 instances across 25 pages**, counting page text, page titles and meta descriptions. They became full stops, commas, colons or brackets, whichever read most naturally. Meaning and facts are unchanged.
- **Definition lists** (for example the glossary on Cybersecurity Basics and the account list on Account Security) now read "Term: definition" instead of "Term, dash, definition".
- **Resource titles** "UK NCSC: Small Business Guide" and "CISA: Cybersecurity for Small Business" use a colon.
- **Threat page titles** now read "Phishing | Threats | CyberSafe SME Nigeria" instead of using a dash.
- **Self-check result message**: "Every basic covered" became "Every item ticked. Revisit this list each quarter. It is a self-check, not an audit." so the result does not suggest a business is secure.

## Left as it was

- All other wording on the older pages. It already read as plain, specific writing with named situations and clear next steps.
- The "not just a credit today" phrase in Payment Security, which is natural in context.
