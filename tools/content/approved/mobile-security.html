Mobile Security

Protect the phone you use for business. Follow practical security steps for iPhone and Android, and learn what to do if your phone is lost or stolen.

iPhone or Android setup Lost your phone?

If your business phone disappeared today, what would someone find on it?

Your smartphone might be where you receive customer orders, confirm payments, speak to suppliers and manage your business WhatsApp.

You may also use it to open business emails, view banking transactions and store photographs of invoices or customer records.

Now imagine leaving that phone in a taxi or discovering that it has been stolen.

The immediate concern is losing the device. But the information and accounts connected to it may be worth far more than the phone itself.

The good news is that both iPhone and Android have security features that can help protect your information. You need to know which ones to enable, how they work and what to do when something goes wrong.

This guide explains the most important settings for both types of phone, with separate instructions where they differ.

Rule to remember

LOCK YOUR PHONE, PROTECT YOUR ACCOUNTS AND PREPARE FOR THE DAY THE DEVICE IS NO LONGER WITH YOU.

Why does your business phone need protection?

Many small businesses now depend on smartphones for everyday operations.

Your phone may hold customer contacts, invoices, business photographs, authentication codes, payment notifications and messages from suppliers.

If it is lost, stolen or compromised, several problems can follow.

  • Business accounts may be exposed. Someone who can unlock the device may try to access connected email, banking or social media accounts.
  • Customer information may be at risk. Contacts, conversations and documents stored on the phone may contain information that should remain private.
  • Your business may lose access to important services. A missing phone can make it harder to receive calls, authenticate account logins or communicate through WhatsApp.
  • A harmful application may misuse information. An application with excessive permissions may collect data or interfere with normal device activity.
  • Business operations may be interrupted. Losing access to orders, records or customer conversations can delay work.

A stolen phone does not automatically mean that someone can read everything on it. A strong device lock, current software and protected accounts can make unauthorised access more difficult.

First, know which phone you are using

This guide covers two mobile operating systems.

iPhone (iOS)

Apple's iPhones use iOS. Security features include Face ID or Touch ID on supported models, Find My, device passcodes and Apple's privacy controls.

Android

Android runs on phones from manufacturers such as Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, Xiaomi, Google and others.

Although these phones share the Android platform, their menus and available features can differ.

For example, a setting shown under Security & privacy on one device may appear under Security, Lock screen or another menu on a different phone.

Throughout this guide, Android instructions describe common routes. If the menu is different, use the search function inside your phone's Settings app and enter the name of the feature.

Some protections also depend on the device model, installed software version and whether Google services are available.

Protect your phone with a strong screen lock

A screen lock is one of the simplest ways to protect business information.

It helps prevent someone from immediately opening your applications or reading your messages when they pick up your phone.

Avoid predictable PINs such as 1234, repeated digits or your date of birth.

A longer, less predictable PIN or password is preferable.

On iPhone (iOS)

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Face ID & Passcode or Touch ID & Passcode, depending on the model.
  3. Enter your existing passcode if prompted.
  4. Select Turn Passcode On or Change Passcode.
  5. Choose a strong passcode. Use Passcode Options if you want a longer numeric or alphanumeric code.
  6. Set up Face ID or Touch ID if your device supports it.

Also open Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock and choose a reasonably short locking period.

On Android

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Look for Security & privacy, Security or Lock screen.
  3. Select Screen lock or the equivalent option.
  4. Choose a PIN or password.
  5. Follow the instructions to complete setup.
  6. Add fingerprint or face unlocking if your phone supports a suitable biometric feature.

A PIN of at least six digits is a sensible starting point. Avoid simple sequences.

Remember: Fingerprints and face recognition can make unlocking convenient, but your underlying PIN or passcode still matters. Do not share it casually with employees or other people.

Keep your phone and applications updated

Software updates do more than introduce new features.

They can also correct security weaknesses that attackers may exploit.

This matters for phones used to access business email, banking and customer information.

On iPhone (iOS)

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select General.
  3. Tap Software Update.
  4. Install any available supported update.
  5. Open Automatic Updates and review the available automatic installation options.

You should also update applications through the App Store.

On Android

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Look for System → Software update or your manufacturer's Software update option.
  3. Check whether a system update is available.
  4. Install supported updates when available.
  5. Check the device's Android security update status.

On some Android phones, the security update information is under Settings → About phone → Android version.

You may also see a separate Google Play system update.

For applications installed through Google Play, open the Play Store, tap your profile icon and select Manage apps & device to review available app updates.

If your phone no longer receives security updates

Consider replacing it when practical, especially if it is used for banking, customer records or sensitive business operations.

Until then, avoid using an unsupported phone for your most sensitive business activities where an alternative is available.

Install applications carefully

An application does not need to look suspicious to put information at risk.

Some harmful applications imitate useful tools, payment services or familiar brands.

Others ask for more permissions than their purpose requires.

On iPhone (iOS)

Use the official App Store and review the developer, application details and permissions before installing an unfamiliar app.

Avoid installing unexpected configuration profiles or device-management settings sent through messages or websites.

Such changes can affect how your device connects to services and how it is managed.

On Android

Use Google Play or another trusted, appropriately verified source supported by your device.

Be cautious with application installation files, often called APK files, received through WhatsApp, Telegram, websites or other unofficial channels.

Where Google Play Protect is available:

  1. Open the Google Play Store.
  2. Tap your profile icon.
  3. Select Play Protect.
  4. Open its settings.
  5. Make sure Scan apps with Play Protect is enabled.

Google Play Protect helps detect potentially harmful applications, but it cannot guarantee that every application is safe.

Avoid disabling security protections merely because a message tells you that an application will not work otherwise.

Review what your applications can access

Your phone allows applications to request access to information and features such as contacts, photographs, location, camera and microphone.

Some permissions are necessary. A delivery application may need your location, while a messaging application may need access to photographs you choose to share.

But you should review whether each application still needs the access it has.

On iPhone (iOS)

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Privacy & Security.
  3. Choose a category such as Location Services, Contacts, Photos, Camera or Microphone.
  4. Review which applications have access.
  5. Change permissions that are unnecessary.

For example, a business application that only needs location information while you are using it may not require continuous location access.

On Android

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Apps.
  3. Choose the application you want to review.
  4. Tap Permissions.
  5. Review or change the permitted access.

On many phones, you can also open Security & privacy → Privacy → Permission manager to review applications by permission type.

The exact route may vary.

Be cautious with applications that request sensitive capabilities such as Accessibility access, notification access or permission to install other applications when those capabilities are unrelated to their purpose.

A useful habit: Before granting a permission, ask whether the application needs that information to perform the task you installed it for.

Prepare your phone for loss or theft

This is one of the most important steps in the guide.

Both iPhone and Android offer tools that may help you locate, secure or erase a missing phone.

These features work best when configured before the phone disappears.

On iPhone: Enable Find My

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap your name at the top of the screen.
  3. Select Find My.
  4. Tap Find My iPhone.
  5. Turn on Find My iPhone.
  6. Review the available Find My network and Send Last Location options.

Find My may help locate your device and allows you to place it in Lost Mode.

On supported devices, the Find My network can assist with locating a device even when it is offline, subject to the feature's requirements.

Additional iPhone protection

On supported iPhones, enable Stolen Device Protection:

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Face ID & Passcode or Touch ID & Passcode.
  3. Enter your passcode.
  4. Select Stolen Device Protection.
  5. Turn it on.

This feature adds protection around certain sensitive actions, particularly if someone steals your iPhone and knows its passcode.

Its availability and behaviour depend on your iOS version, device configuration and settings.

On Android: Check Find Hub

Google's device-finding service is called Find Hub. Some devices or older instructions may still refer to Find My Device.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Search for Find Hub.
  3. Check that device location and finding options are enabled.
  4. If necessary, look under Google → All services → Find Hub or the equivalent security settings.
  5. Confirm that your Google Account is signed in and that the device is eligible for the service.

You can review your devices through Google's official Find Hub service.

Additional Android protection

Some newer Android phones support features such as:

  • Theft Detection Lock: May lock the screen when the phone detects certain movements associated with theft.
  • Offline Device Lock: Can lock the screen after certain suspicious offline conditions.
  • Remote Lock: Lets you request a screen lock using an eligible, previously verified phone number.

On supported devices, these options may be found under Settings → Google → All services → Theft protection.

If a feature is unavailable or greyed out, your device or software may not support it. Do not assume every Android phone offers the same protections.

Back up the information you cannot afford to lose

If your phone is lost, damaged or permanently erased, a recent backup can help you restore supported information.

For business owners, this may include contacts, photographs, device settings and certain application data.

On iPhone (iOS)

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap your name.
  3. Select iCloud.
  4. Tap iCloud Backup.
  5. Turn on Back Up This iPhone.
  6. Use Back Up Now if you want to create a backup immediately.

Check the date of the most recent successful backup.

You can also back up your iPhone using an appropriate computer-based method.

On Android

On many Android devices with Google services:

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Google or Google services.
  3. Open All services, if available.
  4. Find Backup under the backup and restore options.
  5. Confirm that backup is enabled.
  6. Review which data is included and when the last backup completed.

Some Android phones place backup options under System, Accounts and backup or another manufacturer-specific menu.

Important: A device backup does not necessarily include every application or business document.

WhatsApp, business applications, banking services and some other applications may have separate backup or recovery procedures.

For example, moving to a new phone may require restoring an application through its own account or provider process.

Check whether your important business records are backed up separately and whether you can restore them.

Do not depend on a backup you have never verified.

Protect the business accounts connected to your phone

The phone itself is only one part of mobile security.

Your Apple Account or Google Account may be connected to device recovery, backups and other services.

Business email, WhatsApp and banking applications have their own account security settings.

Use strong, unique passwords or passkeys where supported, and enable multi-factor authentication.

Ensure recovery methods are up to date and that you have a way to regain account access without relying entirely on the phone that might be lost.

If employees share a phone for business, decide who is responsible for the device, account recovery and access permissions.

Avoid placing every important business account under the personal email or phone number of one employee without a recovery arrangement.

For more detailed guidance on ownership and permissions, see our Account Security article.

Be careful with public Wi-Fi and shared phones

Business owners often connect to Wi-Fi in restaurants, hotels, markets or other public places.

Use connections you trust when accessing sensitive business systems.

If a network appears suspicious, requests unusual software installation or sends you to an unexpected login page, avoid entering business credentials.

For sensitive activity, mobile data may be a more suitable alternative when available.

Do not automatically assume that a network is safe because its name looks familiar, or unsafe simply because it is public. Pay attention to the website or application you are using and its security warnings.

Avoid conducting business banking on a borrowed or public phone unless it is absolutely necessary and you can follow the provider's secure access procedures.

If you must use another device, do not save credentials, remain signed in or approve unfamiliar account permissions.

LOST YOUR BUSINESS PHONE? WHAT TO DO FIRST

The first response matters

If a phone containing business information is missing, do not focus only on buying a replacement.

Take steps to secure the device, mobile number and connected accounts.

When a business phone goes missing

  1. Phone missingStart the response
  2. Secure deviceFind My / Find Hub
  3. Mobile providerProtect your SIM and number
  4. Business accountsCheck banking and sign-in access
  5. Inform peopleWarn those who may be affected
  6. Recovery or erasureConsider next steps carefully

iPhone: Find Devices and Lost Mode.

Android: Find Hub and available device-securing options.

Remote actions depend on device settings, connection and eligibility. Never confront a suspected thief. Consider remote erasure carefully, as it can affect subsequent location and recovery.

Step 1: Try to locate and secure the device

If you use an iPhone, sign in through Apple's official Find Devices service:

https://www.icloud.com/find

Select the missing device and use Mark as Lost to activate Lost Mode when appropriate.

If you use Android, open Google's Find Hub service:

https://android.com/find

Select the missing device and use the available location or device-securing options.

These features have requirements and may not work immediately if the phone is offline, unsupported or not configured correctly.

Never put yourself in danger by attempting to recover a stolen phone directly from a suspected thief.

Step 2: Contact your mobile network provider

If the phone has been stolen or you believe someone may misuse the SIM, contact your mobile operator through an official support channel.

Ask about suspending the SIM, protecting your number and obtaining a replacement.

This is important because your phone number may receive calls or verification messages used by business accounts.

Step 3: Protect your financial accounts

Contact your bank or payment provider if the phone was used for business banking and you believe access may be at risk.

Ask about securing mobile banking access, active sessions and any suspicious transactions.

If you notice an unauthorised transfer, report it immediately.

Step 4: Secure your other important accounts

From a trusted device, review your business email, Apple Account or Google Account, WhatsApp and other important services.

Change credentials where exposure is suspected, review active sessions and use the providers' official recovery procedures.

If you can no longer access WhatsApp or another service, follow its account-recovery process rather than trusting unofficial recovery agents.

Step 5: Inform the people who need to know

Tell the business owner, relevant employees or trusted service provider if business information may be exposed.

If the missing phone could be used to impersonate your business, warn affected customers or contacts through another trusted channel.

Avoid unnecessarily publishing sensitive details about the incident.

Step 6: Consider remote erasure if recovery is unlikely

Both Apple's Find My and Google's Find Hub provide remote erasure options under supported conditions.

Use this carefully.

Remote erasure can permanently remove information from the device and may affect your ability to continue locating it.

For Android, erasing the device normally stops it appearing as a locatable device in Find Hub.

For iPhone, Apple advises keeping a stolen device associated with Find My so that Activation Lock remains in place, even when remote erasure is necessary.

Before erasing a business-managed phone, consult the person responsible for IT or device management where appropriate.

Step 7: Preserve evidence and report suspected theft or fraud

Keep relevant device details, account alerts, transaction information and any messages associated with the incident.

For suspected cybercrime, the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre provides an online reporting portal:

https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/

For theft, make an appropriate police report. If financial fraud occurred, contact the relevant bank immediately.

The phone that carried the day's orders

Illustrative scenario. This is not an account of a real incident.

Zainab ran a small cake business in Lagos. Customers usually contacted her through WhatsApp Business, where she received cake designs, delivery addresses and payment enquiries.

One Friday afternoon, after returning from a delivery, she realised her phone was missing.

She searched her bag, checked the delivery vehicle and tried calling the number from another phone. Nobody answered.

Her immediate worry wasn't the cost of replacing the device. She had several orders scheduled for the weekend, and most of the customer conversations were on the missing phone.

When she considered signing in to WhatsApp Business on another device, she realised that receiving the verification code could be difficult because the SIM was also missing.

She contacted her mobile provider through an official channel to begin protecting and recovering the number. She also tried to use the device's available remote security features.

Meanwhile, she checked the order details she had written in her notebook and contacted the customers whose deliveries she could confirm.

Some information was still difficult to retrieve, and she couldn't immediately establish whether anyone had attempted to access the missing phone.

The business didn't stop operating completely, but the experience left Zainab struggling to manage orders while also protecting her accounts.

She realised how much of the business depended on one device and began reviewing her backup and account-recovery arrangements.

What this story teaches: Losing a business phone can interrupt customer communication and account access, even when there is no confirmed data breach. Secure device settings, recovery options and protected business records help reduce that disruption.
