SIM Swap Fraud

When your phone goes silent, your money could be at risk.

Check your protection Why it matters

Your phone suddenly shows “No Service.” Would you ignore it?

Sometimes a lost signal is just a network problem. But if your number has unexpectedly stopped working, especially when you haven't changed your SIM or requested a replacement, don't dismiss it without checking.

A SIM swap scam can allow a fraudster to take control of your phone number. Depending on the accounts and security measures involved, this may expose calls, text messages and account-recovery codes intended for you.

For a business owner, that could put banking access, email, social media and customer communications at risk.

What is SIM swap fraud?

A SIM swap happens when a mobile number is transferred to a different SIM card. The process can be legitimate, for example when replacing a damaged SIM. Fraud occurs when someone obtains that transfer without the account holder's authorisation.

A legitimate swap

You replace a damaged SIM, and the number moves to the new card with your authorisation.

A fraudulent swap

Someone obtains the transfer without the account holder's authorisation.

A fraudster may impersonate the number's owner and persuade a telecom provider to issue or activate a replacement SIM.

If the unauthorised transfer succeeds, the victim's original SIM may lose service while calls and text messages for that number go to the replacement SIM.

If the number is linked to banking, email or other important accounts, the attacker may try to use it to intercept authentication messages or reset account access. Whether this succeeds depends on the security controls used by each service.

Why should your business care?

Your phone number may be linked to important accounts

If your business email, banking services or social media accounts rely on that number for verification or recovery, losing control of it could create additional risks.

Fraud can happen without direct access to your phone

An attacker who successfully takes over your number may receive calls and messages intended for you.

Business operations may be disrupted

If customers contact you through your mobile number or WhatsApp Business, losing access can affect orders, supplier communications and customer trust.

The financial consequences can be serious

If an attacker also gains access to a banking or payment account, unauthorised transactions may follow.

Anyone can be affected

You don't have to be a public figure or run a large company for your phone number to be valuable to a fraudster.

How does the scam happen?

Gathers information
Attempts SIM replacement
Original phone loses service
Targets linked accounts
  1. The fraudster gathers informationThey may obtain personal information from public profiles, previous data exposure, deceptive messages or other sources. The information may help them impersonate the phone number's owner.
  2. They attempt an unauthorised SIM replacementThe fraudster contacts the telecom provider or exploits weaknesses in the SIM replacement process to try to move the number to another SIM.
  3. The original phone loses serviceIf the transfer succeeds, the original SIM may stop connecting to the mobile network. The owner may initially assume it is an ordinary network outage.
  4. The attacker targets linked accountsThey may attempt to access services that use the number for authentication or account recovery. A SIM swap does not automatically give them access to every linked account, but it can remove an important layer of protection.

What warning signs should you look for?

Watch for the following:

Loss of service

Your phone suddenly shows “No Service,” “No Network” or “SIM not provisioned” without an obvious explanation.

Missing calls or texts

You stop receiving calls or text messages that other people confirm they sent.

Unexpected SIM notices

You receive unexpected notifications about SIM replacement, registration or account changes.

Unfamiliar bank activity

You see banking alerts or transactions you don't recognise.

Unrequested resets

You receive messages about password resets or account recovery that you didn't request.

An unexplained loss of signal does not prove that a SIM swap has occurred. Network outages, damaged SIM cards and device faults can cause similar symptoms. However, because a fraudulent transfer can have serious consequences, it is worth investigating promptly.

What should you do if you suspect a SIM swap?

Act immediately

  1. Contact your telecom provider.Use an official customer-care channel or visit an authorised service centre. Explain that your number has unexpectedly lost service and ask whether a SIM replacement or transfer was requested. If it was unauthorised, ask the provider to secure the number and explain how to restore your access.
  2. Contact your bank.Call the official fraud line using a number from your bank's website, card or banking app, if accessible. Explain the situation and ask what protective measures can be applied to your accounts.
  3. Check your accounts safely.Use a trusted device and connection to review recent transactions and important account activity. Do not click links in unexpected messages claiming to help restore your service.
  4. Secure linked accounts.Once you have a safe way to access them, review your email, banking, social media and other accounts linked to the affected number. Change exposed passwords, review recovery settings and remove unfamiliar sessions where possible.

If money has already been taken

  • Notify your bank's fraud team immediately and request urgent assistance with the unauthorised transactions.
  • Ask what account restrictions, transaction-tracing or recovery options are available. Do not assume the money can automatically be reversed.
  • Preserve transaction references, alerts, messages and other relevant evidence.
  • Report the incident to the appropriate authorities. In Nigeria, you can consult the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre reporting portal at nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport.
Do not wait until every detail is confirmed before contacting your provider and bank.

How can you reduce the risk?

Protect personal information

Avoid sharing sensitive identity details with unexpected callers or messages claiming to represent your bank or telecom provider.

Ask your provider about extra SIM-swap protection

If a SIM replacement PIN or additional verification option is available, find out how to enable it.

Strengthen important accounts

Use strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.

Avoid relying on SMS alone where alternatives exist

Use an authenticator app, passkey or security key where supported by the service.

Secure account recovery

Keep your recovery email and phone number current, and protect the email account itself with strong authentication.

Save official support contacts

Keep your bank's fraud number and telecom provider's support details somewhere you can access if your phone stops working.

Act on unexpected network loss

If there is no clear explanation, contact your provider promptly, especially if other account warnings appear.
