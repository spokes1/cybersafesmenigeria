Business Security Self-Check

Fifteen practical questions. One useful next step. Review what is in place and what to improve next.

Review habits around accounts, payments, devices, records and people. This is an awareness and planning exercise, not a security audit, risk grade, compliance assessment or security guarantee.

Your answers stay in this browser. Nothing is submitted to CyberSafe. You can use the check even if browser storage is unavailable. Never enter passwords, OTPs or private account details.

1. Accounts and access

A1. Important business accounts use strong, different passwords or passkeys.

Think about email, banking, social media and accounts that store customer information.

Read related guidance

A2. Extra sign-in protection is enabled on important accounts wherever available.

For example, two-step verification for business email and WhatsApp. Follow the options supported by each app.

Read related guidance

A3. Only people who still need access can use our business accounts.

Include employees, former helpers, shared logins, social media access and WhatsApp linked devices. For a one-person business, review all connected sessions.

Read related guidance
2. Payments and scams

P1. Before releasing goods or services, we confirm digital payments through a trusted bank or payment-provider channel.

Do not rely only on a screenshot, SMS alert or message. This includes transfers and payments accepted using POS terminals. Choose “Does not apply” only if your business never accepts digital payments.

Read related guidance

P2. Before paying changed supplier bank details, we verify the change using a contact method we already trust.

For example, call a known supplier number, not just the number in the message requesting the change. Choose “Does not apply” only if your business never pays suppliers by transfer.

Read related guidance

P3. We independently check unexpected requests for money, sign-in codes or links, even when they appear to come from someone familiar.

This includes WhatsApp, SMS, email and social media messages.

Read related guidance
3. Phones and computers

D1. Work phones and computers lock when they are not being used.

Use a PIN, password or biometric lock and avoid leaving a shared business device open.

Read related guidance

D2. Business phones, computers and important apps receive security updates.

If a device or operating system is no longer supported, there is a plan to move to a supported option.

Read related guidance

D3. We use built-in or suitable device protection and install applications from trusted sources.

For example, Windows Security, Google Play Protect, verified app stores or other reputable protection appropriate to the device.

Read related guidance
4. Business information and recovery

R1. We know where important business and customer records are kept and limit unnecessary access.

Examples include customer phone numbers, orders, staff details, invoices and delivery addresses.

Read related guidance

R2. Important business records have a separate, protected backup copy.

The copy should not depend on the same phone or computer that holds the original.

Read related guidance

R3. We have checked that an important file or record can be recovered from a backup.

A backup is more useful when you know a restore actually works.

Read related guidance
5. People and response

S1. Everyone handling business messages or payments knows to pause and report suspicious requests.

If you run the business alone, this includes you. Staff do not need technical knowledge to raise a concern.

Read related guidance

S2. We know the first actions and official contacts to use after suspected fraud or account compromise.

Examples include contacting the bank, mobile operator, account provider or appropriate Nigerian reporting body.

Read related guidance

S3. We occasionally review security practices using a realistic example or recent business change.

This can be a short discussion about a suspicious WhatsApp message, new worker, lost phone or device replacement.

Read related guidance
