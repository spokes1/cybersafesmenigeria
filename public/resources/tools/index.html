Free and Low-Cost Cybersecurity Tools

Practical security features and services for Nigerian small businesses. Start with the phones, laptops and online accounts you already use before installing anything new.

Start with what you already use.

You don't need every security tool. Start with the right one.

A shop owner receives orders through WhatsApp Business, checks transfers in a banking app and saves customer details on an Android phone. A small office uses Gmail, Microsoft Word and a shared Windows laptop. A food business keeps its delivery list in a spreadsheet.

These businesses do not need the same collection of security software. Many useful protections may already be built into their devices and accounts.

The first question is not Which antivirus should I buy? It is What do I need to protect, and what can I use safely today?

Rule to remember: Start with the security features you already have. Add another tool only when you understand the problem it solves.

The tools below are examples, not a ranking, advertisement or promise of protection. Their availability, pricing and features can change. Some are built-in features; others are optional applications or specialist websites.

1. Protect the accounts you already use

Google Account Security Checkup

Official guide: https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/46526

Useful when: Your business uses Gmail, Google Drive, an Android phone or another service connected to a Google Account.

Review your recovery number and email address, devices with access and sign-in protection. Open the security settings of the business-owned account, not a link from an unexpected message.

Start here: Check the recovery options for the Gmail account your business depends on. Make sure a former employee or a single personal phone is not the only way to recover it.

Google Password Manager and built-in password-saving features

Official guide: https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/6197437

Google Password Manager works through supported Android and Chrome environments. Windows, Apple devices and other major browsers also have password-management features.

These features can help create and store distinct passwords. But a password manager does not settle who owns the business accounts. Avoid placing vital shared-business credentials in one employee's personal profile without a proper recovery and handover arrangement.

For a team, review whether a managed business password-sharing service is more appropriate than informal password sharing.

Google Authenticator and Microsoft Authenticator

Official guides: https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/1066447 and https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/authenticator/

These applications provide additional sign-in verification for supported services. Google Authenticator can generate codes without an active internet or mobile-data connection after setup. Microsoft Authenticator is a useful option when a business relies on Microsoft services or another compatible account.

Use the sign-in method the service officially supports. Where passkeys or other stronger options are offered, consider them. Protect recovery codes and understand what happens if the owner's phone is lost.

Never read an authentication code to an unexpected caller, even one claiming to be from your bank or customer support.

Related guides: /learn/password-security, /protect-your-business/account-security and /learn/email-security.

2. Protect the phones and computers you already own

Google Play Protect (supported Android devices)

Official guide: https://developers.google.com/android/play-protect

For a business using a supported Android phone, including commonly used Android brands, Play Protect helps check applications for harmful behaviour. Open the Play Store's Play Protect settings and check whether protection is enabled.

Not every Android device has the same Google services, and not every phone receives updates for the same length of time. Check your own device's update and certification status rather than assuming every Android phone is identical.

Keep the phone locked, install applications from trusted sources, and do not ignore genuine security alerts. Play Protect is one protection layer, not a guarantee that every app is safe.

Windows Security and Microsoft Defender Antivirus

Official guide: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/windows-security/stay-protected-with-the-windows-security-app

Windows Security is built into supported Windows computers and includes Microsoft Defender Antivirus and firewall protections. If your business uses a Windows 11 laptop, check that protection and security updates are enabled before paying for additional antivirus software.

Standard Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025. Businesses still using Windows 10 should plan to move to Windows 11 on compatible hardware, or replace incompatible computers with supported devices.

A completed antivirus scan does not guarantee that a computer is free from compromise. Follow trusted incident-response steps when something genuinely looks wrong.

Find My and Find Hub for lost phones

Official guides: https://support.apple.com/en-us/101593 and https://support.google.com/android/answer/6160491

Apple Find My and Google's Find Hub can help locate, secure or, where supported, remotely erase a missing device. Setup, connectivity and account access matter, so enable and understand the relevant feature before a business phone is lost.

Remote erasure may permanently remove local information. Review the available backups and recovery options before choosing it.

Related guides: /protect-your-business/device-security, /learn/mobile-security and /learn/whatsapp-security.

3. Keep business information recoverable

Google Drive, Microsoft OneDrive and protected offline copies

Official guides: https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2375012 and https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/onedrive

Businesses may already keep quotations, order spreadsheets or invoices in Google Drive or OneDrive. These services can help access and share files, and some files can be prepared for offline use.

Choose a business-controlled account, restrict who can access customer information and review sharing permissions. Plan how staff will work if mobile data or the internet is unavailable.

Important: Cloud synchronisation is not automatically an independent backup. Accidental deletions or unwanted changes may propagate. Keep a separate protected copy where appropriate, secure external storage and occasionally test that a file can actually be restored.

An external storage drive can be part of that plan, but do not leave every backup permanently connected to a computer that might become compromised.

Start here: Restore one non-sensitive test file from a protected backup. Make sure you know whose account or device holds the copy.

Related guides: /learn/backup-and-recovery and /learn/data-protection.

4. Check suspicious account activity and find trusted help

Account activity and security notifications

If you use Google or Microsoft accounts, start with their official account-security settings, not third-party applications promising to find hackers.

Google Security Checkup can help review recommendations and account access. Microsoft's recent-activity features can help users inspect unfamiliar sign-ins for supported account types.

Official guides: https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/46526 and https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/security/what-is-the-recent-activity-page

An unusual location alone does not prove an intruder signed in. Mobile networks and other normal activity may produce unfamiliar locations. Investigate the full context and secure the account when necessary.

Have I Been Pwned (optional)

Official website: https://haveibeenpwned.com/

This website can help check whether an email address has appeared in breach information known to the service. It's useful for business owners ready to investigate possible exposure, but it is not a complete account-security check.

If no record appears, that does not prove the account has never been compromised. Never type your passwords into an unfamiliar website claiming to check data breaches.

Nigerian help and incident reporting

ngCERT: https://cert.gov.ng/ — Nigeria's Computer Emergency Response Team publishes threat guidance and provides official incident-reporting channels.

Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre: https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/ — an official reporting route for suspected cybercrime.

These are public resources, not security apps. If money has been transferred fraudulently, contact your bank through verified channels immediately; do not wait for an online cybercrime report before alerting the bank. Preserve relevant transaction records and messages.

Related guides: /protect-your-business/incident-response and /learn/email-security.

5. More specialist tools: useful in the right situation

Bitwarden (optional password manager)

Official website: https://bitwarden.com/

Bitwarden is an established password manager with applications for supported devices. It can be a useful option when built-in password storage is not enough. Its free individual plan and paid business-management features are not the same thing. Check the features, recovery arrangements and any payment requirements before using it for staff credentials.

No More Ransom (specialist recovery resource)

Official website: https://www.nomoreransom.org/

This resource provides information and decryption tools for some ransomware families. It cannot unlock every affected computer. A business facing ransomware should preserve evidence, isolate affected systems appropriately and seek competent assistance before running unfamiliar recovery tools.

VirusTotal (important privacy warning, not a starter recommendation)

Official website: https://www.virustotal.com/

VirusTotal is used for analysing suspicious files and online indicators, but ordinary public submissions are not confidential. Do not upload customer lists, invoices, identity documents, employment records, passwords or other confidential business files to a public scanning website.

If a file or USB device looks suspicious, stop interacting with it and follow the business's reporting process. A scan result alone is not a guarantee of safety.

Before choosing any tool, ask these questions

  • Can I use it on my actual device? Check the operating system, application availability, version and account requirements.
  • Does it solve a real problem? Choose protection for a specific account, device or record.
  • Is the cost manageable? Understand the difference between a free personal feature and a paid business plan. Avoid assuming that online payment will always be straightforward.
  • Will it work with our connectivity? Some features work offline once set up; others need data or internet access.
  • Who controls it? Business-critical access should not depend only on a departing employee's personal account.
  • Can we recover? Know the recovery steps before losing a phone, password or file.
  • What data must we share? Do not expose sensitive customer records just to test an unfamiliar service.

Start here: one account, one device, one backup

Choose the main email or WhatsApp Business account, the phone or laptop used for work, and one important record. Check the account's sign-in protection, the device's built-in protections and whether the record can be restored from a trusted backup.
