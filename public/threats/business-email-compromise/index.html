Business email compromise

The most expensive attacks on SMEs are usually quiet, patient and grammatically correct.

Business email compromise, often shortened to BEC, is what happens after someone gets into a business email account and decides to read rather than shout. They watch a genuine conversation about a genuine invoice, then step in at the right moment with new account details.

There is no malware to detect and no obvious warning. The email arrives in the correct thread, signed off the way that person always signs off.

How the setup works

  1. A password is captured through phishing, or reused from a breach elsewhere.
  2. The attacker logs in and creates a quiet mailbox rule that hides or forwards certain messages.
  3. They wait for a payment discussion — a supplier invoice, a rent payment, a project milestone.
  4. They reply with updated bank details, sometimes from a near-identical domain instead of the real one.

Signs worth checking

  • Bank details that change late in a transaction, especially with an apology about a "frozen" old account.
  • Replies that arrive from a domain missing or adding one character.
  • Mailbox rules, forwarding addresses or recovery emails nobody remembers creating.
  • Sign-in activity from unfamiliar locations or devices in your email security settings.

Controls that stop it

  1. Two-factor authentication on every business email account, without exception.
  2. Verbal confirmation on a known number for every change of payment details.
  3. A second approver for transfers above a threshold you set.
  4. A monthly look at mailbox rules, forwarding and connected devices.
  5. Immediate password changes and session sign-outs when staff leave.
