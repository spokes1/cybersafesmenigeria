A quick tip to protect your business

Verify payments before releasing goods

Don’t rely on a screenshot or SMS alert as proof of payment. Check your bank account or transaction history through your normal banking channel before releasing goods or confirming a service.

Pause before clicking suspicious links

An urgent email, unexpected prize or message requesting account verification could be a phishing attempt. Check the sender and website address independently before clicking or sharing sensitive information.

Secure your business WhatsApp

Enable two-step verification, protect your phone with a screen lock and never share your WhatsApp verification code. Be cautious if someone claiming to be a colleague suddenly asks for money or sensitive information.

Use strong, unique passwords

Avoid reusing one password across your email, banking and business accounts. Use a different, strong password for each account and turn on multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.

Back up important business files

Keep copies of essential invoices, customer records and business documents in a secure backup location. Make backups regularly and test that you can restore your files.

Threats

Know the warning signs before they cost you.

Fraud can begin with an ordinary-looking message, a changed supplier account number or a request that feels urgent. Learn what to check before you act.

Phishing

A message that looks genuine but is designed to trick you into revealing information, opening a harmful link or sending money.

Read more

Fake bank alerts

A false payment notification can persuade staff to release goods before the money has actually arrived. Confirm payments through your normal banking channel before releasing goods or services.

Read more

WhatsApp hijacking

Once an account is taken over, attackers can use familiar conversations to ask customers, suppliers or colleagues for money or codes.

Read more

SIM swap fraud

Someone takes control of your phone number and tries to use it to reach your bank, email or other accounts.

Read more

Malware

Malicious software can steal information, damage systems or interrupt business operations.

Read more

Social engineering

Instead of breaking into a system, criminals may simply convince someone to act, such as sharing a code or paying into a “new” supplier account.
