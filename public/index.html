Quick facts

Did You Know?

Three numbers that explain why cybersecurity is a business issue, not just an IT one.

📊

Most attacks start with a human

Over 74% of all data breaches involve a human element — such as clicking a phishing link, using a weak password, or falling for a social engineering attack. Awareness training is the single highest-return investment any business can make.

Source: Verizon DBIR 2025
💸

Small businesses pay more per record

Small businesses pay an average of $164 per compromised record in a data breach — often more than large enterprises — because they lack the insurance coverage, legal teams, and recovery infrastructure that larger companies have.

Source: IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2024
🔐

2FA blocks 99% of automated attacks

Enabling Two-Factor Authentication on your accounts blocks more than 99% of automated credential attacks, according to Microsoft research. It is free, takes under two minutes to set up, and is the single most effective account protection step available.

Source: Microsoft Security Research

Defense playbook

How to protect your business

Actionable, step-by-step guidance to safeguard your operational data and customer trust.

Each section below covers a specific protection area. Click to expand and read the step-by-step guidance. Start with Password Safety if you are new to cybersecurity.

01

Password Safety

+

Use passwords at least 12 characters long. Include uppercase, lowercase, numbers and symbols. Never reuse passwords. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on every account. Use a password manager.

02

Spot a Phishing Attack

+

Check the sender's email carefully. Look for urgent language designed to panic you. Hover over links before clicking. Never share OTPs or passwords over chat. When in doubt, call the sender directly to verify.

03

Secure Your Mobile Phone

+

Lock your screen with a PIN or biometrics. Only install apps from Google Play Store or Apple App Store. Keep your apps and operating system updated. Enable Find My Device. Back up your data every week.

04

After a Cyber Incident

+

Disconnect affected devices from the internet immediately. Change all compromised passwords. Contact your bank if financial accounts are affected. Restore from your most recent clean backup. Report to ngCERT at cert.gov.ng.

Quick cyber hygiene tips for staff

  • Never plug unknown USB drives into business computers.
  • Verify unexpected bank transfer requests over phone call.
  • Lock screens when stepping away from desks.
  • Use separate devices or profiles for personal and business use.

Got questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the security questions Nigerian business owners ask us most.

01

What is phishing and how do I recognise it?

+

Phishing is when a criminal sends a fake message — by email, SMS, or WhatsApp — designed to look like it came from a trusted source such as your bank, NIMC, or a delivery company. Red flags include urgent language ("Your account will be suspended"), requests for your password or OTP, links with unusual domain names, and messages asking you to act immediately. When in doubt, call the organisation directly using a number from their official website.

02

Can ransomware affect a small business in Nigeria?

+

Yes. Ransomware does not discriminate by business size. It typically enters through a malicious email attachment, an infected USB drive, or an unpatched software vulnerability. Once inside, it encrypts your files and demands payment — usually in cryptocurrency — to restore access. The most effective protection is regular offline backups, updated software, and staff training.

03

How do I create a strong password I can actually remember?

+

Use a passphrase — three or four unrelated words joined together with numbers and symbols. For example: Coffee#Lagos7Bridge! is both strong and memorable. Alternatively, use a free password manager like Bitwarden to generate and store complex passwords automatically, so you only need to remember one master password.

04

My business WhatsApp was hacked. What should I do immediately?

+

Act immediately: (1) Open WhatsApp on your phone, go to Settings → Linked Devices, and log out all active sessions. (2) Re-enable Two-Factor Authentication in WhatsApp Settings → Account → Two-Step Verification. (3) Notify your customers through another channel that your account was compromised and that they should ignore any messages sent from it during the period. (4) Report to ngCERT at cert.gov.ng.

05

How do I tell if a bank alert is real or fake?

+

Never trust an alert until you verify it in your banking app directly. Open your official banking app — not from a link in the message — and check your actual account balance and transaction history. Fake alerts can look identical to real ones. The only reliable confirmation is your bank's app or a call to your bank's official number.

06

Do I need antivirus software if I only use my phone for business?

+

Smartphones are not immune to malware. While iOS and Android have built-in protections, they can still be compromised through malicious apps, phishing links, and unsecured Wi-Fi. On Android, install a reputable antivirus app from the Google Play Store. On both platforms, always keep your operating system updated — most mobile security patches arrive through system updates.

07

What is the 3-2-1 backup rule and how do I apply it to my business?

+

The 3-2-1 rule means: keep 3 copies of your important data, stored on 2 different types of media (such as your laptop and an external drive), with 1 copy stored offsite or in the cloud (such as Google Drive or Dropbox). For a small business, this could be as simple as: your working files on your laptop, a weekly copy on an external drive, and an automatic sync to Google Drive.

08

How do I report a cybercrime in Nigeria?

+

You have three official channels: (1) ngCERT — Nigeria's national cyber emergency response team — at cert.gov.ng handles cyber incidents. (2) The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) handles online fraud and financial cybercrime at efcc.gov.ng. (3) The Nigeria Police Force Cybercrime Unit can be reached through your nearest police station. Always preserve evidence — screenshots, messages, transaction records — before reporting.
