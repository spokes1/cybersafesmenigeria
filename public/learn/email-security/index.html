How to Secure Your Business Email

Email is the master key to your business. Almost every other account can be reset from it.

If an attacker gets one account from your business, they want this one. From inside your mailbox they can reset other services, read your invoices, learn your suppliers, and reply inside a real conversation about a real payment. Everything else on this page follows from that.

Do these five things first

  1. Turn on two-factor authentication.In Gmail: Manage your Google Account → Security → 2-Step Verification. In Microsoft 365: Security info → Add sign-in method. Use an authenticator app rather than SMS where you can.
  2. Give the account its own password.Not a version of one you use elsewhere.
  3. Check for mailbox rules you did not create.This is the step almost everyone skips. Gmail: Settings → Filters and blocked addresses, and Settings → Forwarding. Microsoft 365: Settings → Mail → Rules, and Forwarding. Attackers leave a rule that quietly hides or copies messages about payments.
  4. Review recent sign-in activity.Both Google and Microsoft show where and on what device your account has been used. End anything unfamiliar.
  5. Check your recovery email and phone number.If either belongs to someone else, they own the account, password or not.

Use a business domain if you can

A mailbox at your own domain (accounts@yourbusiness.com.ng rather than a free personal address) lets you control accounts centrally, remove access when staff leave, and set the DNS records (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) that make it harder for anyone to send email pretending to be you. Your domain registrar or hosting provider can set these up.

Reading email safely

  • Check the actual sender address, not the display name. The display name is free text and can say anything.
  • Hover over links, or press and hold on mobile, and read the destination before tapping.
  • Be suspicious of attachments you did not expect, especially anything that asks you to enable macros or content.
  • A reply that arrives from a domain missing one letter is the single most expensive email trick used against Nigerian SMEs.

Rules for the team

  • Every staff member gets their own mailbox. No shared inbox passwords.
  • Any change of bank details, from anyone, is confirmed by phone on a known number before payment.
  • Report suspicious mail internally the same day. These campaigns hit several colleagues at once.
  • When someone leaves: change the password, end all sessions, and remove their access the same day.

If an account was already compromised

  1. Change the password from a device you trust, then sign out of all sessions.
  2. Delete any forwarding address or rule the attacker created.
  3. Reset the recovery email and phone number.
  4. Warn anyone who was in an active payment conversation with that mailbox.
  5. Report to ngCERT (opens in a new tab), and to your bank if payment details were discussed.
