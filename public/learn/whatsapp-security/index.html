How to Protect Your Business WhatsApp

For a lot of businesses here, WhatsApp is the shop. Treat it with the same care as the cash box.

Your WhatsApp holds order history, customer numbers, price lists and the trust of everyone you have ever sold to. That last part is what attackers are really after: a payment request from your own number gets paid.

Set-up that prevents most takeovers

  1. Two-step verification.Settings → Account → Two-step verification. Set a six-digit PIN and add a recovery email you control. This alone blocks the common code-stealing takeover.
  2. Never share the six-digit code.No customer, courier, bank or "WhatsApp support" needs it. Anyone asking is taking your account.
  3. Check linked devices weekly.Settings → Linked devices. Log out anything you do not recognise, and after using WhatsApp Web on a shared computer.
  4. Lock the phone.A screen PIN or biometrics, plus a SIM PIN so a stolen phone cannot move your number to another handset.
  5. Use WhatsApp Business on a dedicated line.It separates business from personal, and gives you labels, catalogues and away messages.

Habits for the sales conversation

  • Publish your account details once, in your business profile, so customers have a fixed reference.
  • Tell customers plainly, in your profile or a pinned message, that your account number never changes and that you will never ask them to send money to a different one.
  • Confirm large or unusual orders with a phone call.
  • Do not accept a payment screenshot as proof. Check your own balance.
  • Be careful with group links — anyone who joins can see the members list, which is a ready-made target list.

If your account is taken over

  1. Re-register your number on your own phone. The new verification logs the attacker out.
  2. Open Settings → Linked devices and end every session.
  3. Re-enable two-step verification immediately.
  4. Post on Instagram, Facebook or SMS that your WhatsApp was compromised and that customers should ignore any payment request from it during that window.
  5. Report to ngCERT (opens in a new tab), and keep screenshots in case customers lost money.
The fastest way to lose a customer's money is a request that looks exactly like you. Give them one fixed account number and repeat it often.
