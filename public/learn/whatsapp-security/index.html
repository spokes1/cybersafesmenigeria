Your business runs on WhatsApp. Who controls the account?

Keep your WhatsApp Business number, conversations and connected devices under control, from the first customer message to the end of the working day.

One number · Known devices · Clear daily routine

A customer sends a picture of the shoes they want. Another asks when their delivery will arrive. A supplier shares a price list, and an employee replies from WhatsApp Web on the shop computer.

By the end of the day, the business may have handled dozens of messages without opening a formal sales system.

But who controls the number customers recognise? Which computers are still linked? If the person handling messages leaves, can the owner still access the business conversations and outstanding orders?

These questions matter just as much as preventing someone from stealing a verification code.

This guide helps you set up safer everyday routines for the WhatsApp Business app, even if you run the business with one phone and no IT department.

RULE TO REMEMBER

KNOW WHO CONTROLS YOUR BUSINESS NUMBER, PROTECT SIGN-INS AND REVIEW EVERY DEVICE THAT CAN READ YOUR MESSAGES.

What does WhatsApp security mean for a small business?

WhatsApp security is not just keeping a six-digit registration code secret. It means protecting the phone number, the device, account recovery, customer conversations and access from linked computers or phones.

For many small businesses, WhatsApp Business is a customer-service desk, order book and communication channel. Its features can help organise conversations, but they do not automatically provide a complete staff-permission system or replace proper business records.

It helps to think about three moments in every working day: before opening, while serving customers and before closing.

Before opening: make sure the business owns its access

Know who controls the phone number

Write down which number is used for business enquiries, who is authorised to manage it and how the SIM or eSIM is maintained.

If the business number belongs to an employee personally, agree on what would happen if the person left or lost their phone. Avoid assuming that knowing the number means the business can regain access whenever it wants.

A dedicated business number can make ownership and handover simpler, but it is not compulsory for every small business. Any arrangement should be clear and agreed, especially when personal phones are involved.

Use the genuine, updated application

Install WhatsApp Business through the phone's official application store. Keep the app and operating system updated, and avoid modified or unofficial WhatsApp clients.

Using WhatsApp Business is not the same as using the larger WhatsApp Business Platform; some team-management capabilities depend on the product and subscription available to the business.

Protect registration and two-step verification

Open WhatsApp Business → Settings → Account → Two-step verification and follow the choices shown in your current app.

WhatsApp has been upgrading two-step verification from a six-digit PIN to a stronger password. Depending on your app version and rollout, you may see a PIN, a password or additional authentication choices. Choose a strong option, store it securely and follow the current on-screen instructions.

Add and verify a recovery email address the business can continue to access. Protect that email account with its own strong sign-in security.

Never tell anyone a registration code or two-step verification secret. A caller claiming to work for WhatsApp, a courier or a customer does not need your code.

Important: A recovery email is useful, but it is not a guaranteed replacement for access to the registered phone number or SIM during account recovery.

Official guidance: two-step verification and email recovery limitations.

Secure the phone itself

Use a strong screen lock, keep software updated and enable supported device-finding features. Where available, enable an additional app lock for WhatsApp Business.

Don't leave the phone unlocked on a counter where customers, delivery riders or temporary staff can use it. When an employee needs the phone for work, agree on what they may access and how they will return it.

While serving customers: make messages safer

Verify instructions that change money or account access

A message may arrive from a familiar customer or supplier asking for a payment change, verification code, unusual refund or link to a new invoice.

Do not decide solely from a profile photo, familiar name or existing chat history. If the request is unusual, verify it through a contact method your business already trusts.

A payment screenshot in WhatsApp is not confirmation of bank credit. Confirm incoming funds through the business's own approved banking channel before releasing goods.

Handle customer records with care

A WhatsApp conversation may contain phone numbers, addresses, photographs, order details and payment references. Share only the information needed for a task.

A delivery rider normally needs the details of assigned deliveries, not an exported list of every customer. Avoid copying whole chat histories or contact lists into personal groups or unapproved storage.

For important orders, keep a suitable business record outside a single staff member's chat history. Protect those records and explain to customers how their information is used, where required.

Be cautious with files, QR codes and unusual links

Customers may send artwork, menus, catalogues or delivery documents. Unexpected files and links should be checked before opening, particularly when they request an app installation, payment or account login.

Do not scan a QR code to 'verify' your WhatsApp account unless you have independently established what it will do. A QR code used to link WhatsApp Web or another device may give that device ongoing access to conversations.

Keep your business identity consistent

Use a recognisable business profile name, accurate contact information and a consistent process for confirming payment instructions.

Tell customers how to reach the business through an established channel if they receive an unexpected request. A professional-looking profile or verification badge can help with recognition but should never replace independent verification of unusual financial requests.

The linked-device check: who else can read the chats?

WhatsApp Business can be used on compatible linked computers and companion devices. This is convenient for staff, but it means a person may still have access after leaving a desk or ending a working arrangement.

On an Android primary phone, open WhatsApp Business, use the three-dot menu and select Linked devices. On an iPhone, open WhatsApp Business Settings → Linked Devices. Menu labels may vary slightly with version.

Review the devices shown. If a computer or phone is unfamiliar, no longer used or associated with someone who should not have access, select it and Log out.

Don't link WhatsApp to a public computer, and avoid leaving sessions active on a shared office computer. A linked session is access to business communications; it is not automatically a restricted staff role.

Where the business needs several employees to handle chats, review the supported options and access model for the product you actually use rather than assuming each linked device has separate permissions.

Official guidance: WhatsApp Business linked devices.

Before closing: leave the account safe for tomorrow

At the end of a shift, the responsible person should check three things.

1. Access: Is the primary phone secure? Were any temporary computer sessions used? Are any linked devices still connected that should not be?

2. Orders: Have paid, pending and disputed orders been recorded in the approved business system or order book? Would someone else know which orders need attention tomorrow without opening a worker's personal account?

3. Recovery: Is the business's recovery email still accessible, and is there a recent, protected backup where the business needs one?

These are useful habits, not a demand that every employee perform a technical audit each evening. The owner can decide what should be checked daily and what can be reviewed weekly.

Protect chats and backups without losing access

Open Settings → Chats → Chat backup to check the available backup options and the date of the last successful backup.

WhatsApp offers end-to-end encrypted backups, with protection methods that may include a password, encryption key or supported passkey. If you enable encrypted backups, keep the necessary recovery method secure. Losing the password or key can prevent restoration.

A cloud backup is not the same as a verified complete business record. It may not include every recent conversation, and restoring it may depend on access to the original phone number and associated cloud account.

For critical orders, invoices and customer commitments, maintain appropriately protected records outside chat history. Follow your business's privacy and retention responsibilities.

Official guidance: encrypted chat backups and recovery methods.

When someone stops working for the business

Suppose an assistant has used WhatsApp Web on a shop computer, or a freelancer has helped with customer enquiries.

Before the working arrangement ends, identify which devices and business tools the person used. Preserve legitimate business records needed for handover, then remove access that is no longer authorised.

Review linked devices, the primary phone, recovery arrangements and any separate business-management tools. Change shared credentials where relevant, without accessing the person's private accounts or devices without appropriate authority.

If your business relies on an employee's personal telephone number, resolve access and ownership arrangements before the handover becomes urgent.

This is not an accusation that former employees intend harm. It is ordinary account management.

If you believe the WhatsApp account has been taken over

If you still control the phone number: Follow WhatsApp's official account-recovery instructions. Re-registering the number on your own device with the required code can end unauthorised sessions, but additional verification requirements or waiting periods may apply. Then review linked devices, two-step verification and recovery settings.

If the phone or SIM is missing: Contact the mobile operator immediately through an official channel to protect or replace the number. Secure the device and connected accounts from another trusted device where possible. Do not assume email alone will restore access to a lost number.

If customers may have received false requests: Use another established channel to warn them and explain how to verify genuine business messages. If money has been transferred, contact the bank promptly and preserve the relevant messages and transaction details.

Do not pay someone who promises to 'hack back' the account or recover it for a fee. Use official recovery channels and seek qualified support where needed.

For the detailed threat explanation, see WhatsApp Hijacking and SIM Swap Fraud rather than treating this page as a complete incident-response guide.

ILLUSTRATIVE · NOT A REPORTED INCIDENT

A short business-desk example

Illustrative example, not a reported incident.

A small cosmetics shop in Lagos uses WhatsApp Business on the owner's phone and WhatsApp Web on a counter laptop. During a busy sales day, an assistant signs in on a borrowed laptop to reply to customers while the counter computer is being repaired.

Two days later, the owner notices that the borrowed laptop is still listed under Linked Devices. She cannot establish whether anyone else opened the session, but she logs it out and checks the business conversations for unexpected activity.

The shop introduces a simple rule: staff must use approved devices for customer messages and report any temporary linked session before leaving work.

The lesson is not that a breach definitely happened. It is that convenience can create access the business forgets to remove.
