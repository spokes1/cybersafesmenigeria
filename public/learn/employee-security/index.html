Employee Security

Your staff are not the weak link. They are the control that works, if you tell them what to expect.

Most staff mistakes are reasonable decisions made with missing information. Someone paid an invoice because it looked normal. Someone read out a code because the caller sounded official. The fix is not suspicion — it is a small number of clear rules, and permission to slow down.

Day one, for every new person

  • Their own login for every system. No shared accounts.
  • A standard user account, not an administrator account.
  • The payment rule, said out loud: any change of account details is confirmed by phone on a known number.
  • The code rule: nobody in this business ever asks you for an OTP or a password. Anyone who does is not us.
  • Who to tell, immediately, if something feels wrong — and the assurance that reporting early is never punished.

The last day matters as much

  1. Change passwords on anything they had access to, and sign out all sessions.
  2. Remove them from the business email, cloud storage, social page admins and payment platforms.
  3. Remove their WhatsApp Business linked devices and any group admin rights.
  4. Recover company phones, laptops and USB drives, and note what was returned.

Keeping awareness alive without a training budget

  • Once a quarter, spend ten minutes on a real message the business actually received. Recognition beats policy.
  • When an attempt is caught, share it — including who caught it.
  • Keep the rules to a single page and put it where people work, not in a shared drive nobody opens.
  • Do not blame the person who clicked. The next person will hide it, and hidden incidents are the expensive ones.

Access, kept small on purpose

Give each role the access it needs and no more. A sales assistant does not need the payment platform; a delivery rider does not need the customer database. When an account is compromised, the damage is limited to whatever that account could reach.
