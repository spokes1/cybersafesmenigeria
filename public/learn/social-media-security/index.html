Social Media Security

Protect your Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp Business accounts, manage access and help customers recognise fraudulent messages and fake pages.

Platform security guides Hijacked or fake account?

Your business has a digital shopfront. Who can customers trust behind it?

A customer sees a product on Instagram, asks about the price through WhatsApp and checks your Facebook Page before placing an order.

For many Nigerian businesses, this is an ordinary sales process.

But what happens when someone takes over one of those accounts, sends customers false payment instructions or creates another profile pretending to be your business?

Social media security is about protecting the accounts you use, managing who can access them and making it easier for customers to identify genuine business communications.

This guide focuses on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp Business, with links to official security guidance for other platforms.

Rule to remember

PROTECT WHO CAN ACCESS YOUR BUSINESS ACCOUNTS, AND VERIFY UNUSUAL REQUESTS BEFORE MONEY OR INFORMATION CHANGES HANDS.

Why should your business care?

Your social media and messaging accounts may contain customer conversations, orders, contact information and advertising arrangements.

If someone gains unauthorised access, they could interfere with customer messages, change business information or misuse the account to impersonate you.

However, someone does not need to access your real account to cause problems. They may create a separate profile using your name, photographs and branding.

Protecting your business therefore involves three responsibilities: securing the genuine accounts, managing the people who use them and helping customers recognise impersonation.

Secure your Facebook business Page

Facebook Pages may be managed directly or through Meta Business Suite.

The important thing is knowing who has permission to act on behalf of the business.

Check your Page access

  1. Sign in through the official Facebook application or website.
  2. Open the business Page and its settings.
  3. Look for Page access, where available.
  4. Review who has full control and who has more limited permissions.
  5. Remove unnecessary access only after confirming that an authorised person will retain control.

If you use Meta Business Suite, open https://business.facebook.com/ and review the relevant business portfolio's people, permissions and connected assets.

Facebook Page access and business portfolio permissions are not the same thing.

An employee who posts content may not need full control of the Page or business portfolio.

Also secure the personal Facebook accounts used by authorised Page managers. If one of those accounts is compromised, the business Page may be affected.

Remember: Keep the business under the control of appropriately authorised people, even when an agency or freelancer manages its daily content.

Secure your Instagram business account

Instagram is often used to display products, attract customers and receive enquiries.

Protecting it starts with its sign-in settings.

Review your account security

  1. Open the official Instagram application.
  2. Go to your profile and open the settings menu.
  3. Find Accounts Centre or the equivalent account-management area.
  4. Open Password and security.
  5. Review your password, two-factor authentication and recent login activity.
  6. Check that your recovery email address and phone number are correct.

If your business uses Meta Business Suite, also review any Instagram-related permissions assigned through the business portfolio.

Avoid routinely sharing the main Instagram password with employees or marketing agencies when supported permission-based alternatives are available.

Be cautious about direct messages claiming that your account will be disabled unless you open a link or provide a verification code.

Open Instagram directly to investigate unexpected account notices rather than relying on a link in a suspicious message.

Remember: A professional-looking profile, familiar sender or verification badge does not make every request trustworthy.

Secure your WhatsApp Business account

WhatsApp Business deserves special attention because customers may use it to place orders, confirm deliveries and discuss payments.

Your business may also depend on one phone number for regular customer communication.

Enable account verification protection

  1. Open WhatsApp Business.
  2. Go to Settings → Account → Two-step verification.
  3. Follow the available instructions to enable additional account protection.
  4. Add or confirm an accessible recovery email address where supported.
  5. Protect your phone and the email account used for recovery.

WhatsApp is updating its two-step verification options. Depending on your application version, you may see a PIN, password or other supported security method. Follow the official instructions shown in your application.

Never share a registration code, verification code or account-protection credential with someone who unexpectedly requests it.

Review linked devices

On Android, open the WhatsApp Business menu and select Linked devices.

On iPhone, open WhatsApp Business Settings → Linked Devices.

Review connected computers and companion phones. Log out any device that you do not recognise or that is no longer authorised.

If employees handle customer chats through connected devices, review these connections when their responsibilities change or they leave the business.

Linked devices are not the same as full account ownership or separate employee permissions. Do not assume every WhatsApp Business setup supports individually assigned staff roles.

Protect business conversations

Avoid sharing customer information in unnecessary groups or allowing unauthorised employees to access sensitive discussions.

If someone asks a customer to pay a different bank account through WhatsApp, verify the instruction independently.

Remember: Control the phone number, recovery arrangements and linked devices associated with your business WhatsApp account.

The controls available to you depend on your account configuration and the platform's current features.

Make your genuine accounts easier to identify

Customers should not have to guess which profile belongs to your business.

Keep your business name, contact details and official links consistent across the platforms you use.

Where possible, publish your genuine handles on your website, invoices, receipts and established customer communication channels.

Make your payment procedure clear. If customers receive unexpected changes to bank details, encourage them to verify those changes using a trusted business contact.

Do not ask customers to disclose confidential information publicly or send verification codes through direct messages.

Warning signs that deserve attention

Investigate if you notice:

  • Posts, stories or messages nobody authorised.
  • Customers reporting unfamiliar payment instructions.
  • Unknown Page administrators, linked devices or account sessions.
  • Unexpected changes to contact information or recovery settings.
  • Login or registration alerts you did not initiate.
  • Another account using your business name, product photographs or logo.

One unusual event does not automatically prove fraud. Check what happened before making an accusation.

Hijacked account or impersonation account?

The difference determines what you should do next.

Someone has accessed your real account

This is an account takeover or hijacking concern.

  1. Use the official application or website to secure the account.
  2. Review active sessions, linked devices and permissions.
  3. Remove unauthorised access where possible and protect recovery methods.
  4. Check for fraudulent messages, changed settings or financial activity.
  5. If you have lost access, use the platform's official recovery process.

Official recovery entry points include https://www.facebook.com/hacked, https://www.instagram.com/hacked/ and WhatsApp's official Help Centre at https://faq.whatsapp.com/1131652977717250/.

For WhatsApp, recovering a taken-over account may require re-registering your number using its official verification process. Simply changing a security password may not remove the person using the account.

Someone has created a fake account pretending to be your business

This is an impersonation concern.

  1. Preserve the suspicious account's URL, username and relevant evidence.
  2. Report the account through the platform's impersonation-reporting tools.
  3. Inform customers using a genuine channel you still control.
  4. Confirm your official handles and normal payment procedure.
  5. Ask affected customers to contact their bank promptly if they have sent money to a fraudulent recipient.

Do not assume that a fake profile means your real account has been hacked.

If suspicious activity involves financial loss or cybercrime, preserve evidence and report it to the relevant provider or authorities. Nigeria's National Cybercrime Centre provides a reporting portal at https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/.

Account recovery, removal of fake profiles and recovery of funds cannot be guaranteed.

The Instagram shop that wasn't theirs

Illustrative scenario. This is not an account of a real incident.

Nneka ran a home-decoration business in Port Harcourt, where she advertised curtains, cushions and household accessories through Instagram.

One afternoon, a regular customer sent her a WhatsApp message.

“Have you changed your account number? I saw your new page, but the payment details are different.”

Nneka hadn't opened another Instagram page or changed the business's payment information.

The customer sent a screenshot. At first glance, the profile looked familiar. It used photographs from Nneka's business and a name almost identical to her official Instagram handle.

But the account was not hers.

Nneka checked her genuine business page and found no obvious signs that someone had accessed it. The immediate concern was a separate account pretending to represent her shop.

She saved the suspicious profile details and reported it through Instagram's impersonation-reporting process.

She also used her established Instagram and WhatsApp Business channels to remind customers which account belonged to the business and how to confirm payment instructions.

A few customers asked questions, and Nneka spent much of the afternoon responding to them instead of processing orders.

She couldn't confirm whether anyone had already sent money to the fake account, and she didn't know how quickly the platform would act on her report.

What this story teaches: Criminals can imitate a business without accessing its genuine accounts. Owners need to protect their accounts and make it easy for customers to recognise their official communication channels.

Three checks to make today

Check account access: Who can manage your Facebook Page, Instagram account and WhatsApp Business devices?

Check security settings: Are additional authentication and recovery options properly configured?

Check customer verification: Can customers easily identify your genuine accounts and confirm payment instructions?
