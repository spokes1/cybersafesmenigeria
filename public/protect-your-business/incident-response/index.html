Incident Response

The first hour matters more than a perfect understanding of what happened. Contain, then investigate.

This page is for the moment you realise something is wrong: an account is behaving oddly, money has left, files will not open, or a customer says they received a strange message from you.

First hour, in order

  1. Contain.Disconnect an affected computer from the network. Do not switch it off if ransomware is involved and you may need evidence, but take it off Wi-Fi and unplug any external drives.
  2. Cut access.Change the password for the affected account from a device you trust, then sign out of all sessions. Do this for email first.
  3. Stop the money.If any payment is involved, call your bank now and ask them to flag or recall it. Speed is the only advantage you have.
  4. Preserve evidence.Screenshots, message threads, email headers, transaction references, times. Do not delete the messages.
  5. Tell the people affected.Staff, and any customer or supplier in an active payment conversation. A short honest message on another channel prevents a second loss.

Then, the same day

  1. Check the compromised mailbox for forwarding rules, filters and new recovery addresses.
  2. Reset passwords anywhere the same password was used.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication if it was not already on.
  4. Review who else has access, and remove anyone who should not.
  5. Restore from backup if data was lost, working on a clean device.

Where to report in Nigeria

  • ngCERT (opens in a new tab): the national computer emergency response team, for cyber incidents and advisories.
  • EFCC (opens in a new tab): for online fraud and financial cybercrime.
  • Nigeria Police Force cybercrime unit: through your nearest station, with your evidence prepared.
  • NDPC (opens in a new tab): if personal data belonging to customers or staff was exposed, there are notification duties under the NDPA.
  • Your bank's fraud desk: as early as possible, in writing as well as by phone.

Write it down afterwards

One page: what happened, how it got in, what you changed, what you would do differently. Businesses that do this rarely get caught by the same method twice. Keep the contacts above, plus your bank's fraud line, on a printed sheet, because an incident is the worst time to look them up.

Nobody should be punished for reporting an incident early. Hidden incidents are the ones that become expensive.
