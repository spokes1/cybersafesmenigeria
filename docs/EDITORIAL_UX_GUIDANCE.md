# CyberSafe editorial and UX variety standard — for future content

**Decision:** Site identity and accessibility stay consistent, but **article structure, page rhythm, teaching method and animations should vary by topic**. The four approved animation batches are complete; this is **future-facing guidance**, not a request to rebuild them.

## Fixed foundation

- Brand: light-only interface, Plus Jakarta Sans; navy `#12304d`, teal `#0f766e` to `#14b8a6`, blue `#1d6fa5`, orange `#e8590c` for warnings only; approved typography, spacing and icon system.
- Audience: Nigerian traders, shop owners, service businesses and small teams. Plain, conversational **British English**, concrete decisions, realistic steps, no em dashes, no exaggerated fear.
- Security accuracy: distinguish suspected from confirmed attacks, real from **illustrative** incidents, verified sources from unverified notes; no invented statistics, zero guarantees of recovery or security.
- Accessible visuals: **user-controlled where interactive**, all important advice available without JS, keyboard focus/labels, screen-reader meaning, reduced-motion support, 320px readability, minimal CLS/performance overhead.
- Maintain existing header search, navigation, routes, global styles, self-check, approved copy and other page functions.

## Editorial composition guidelines

1. **Vary the opening.** Sometimes begin with a short Nigerian workplace conversation, sometimes a decision, a concrete mistake, a simple definition or a visual explainer. Do not mechanically begin every page with “Imagine...” or end each with an identical list.
2. **Avoid one universal article template.** Use a different combination of short sections, comparison, numbered actions, scenario, decision flow, troubleshooting, quick-reference or micro-exercise as appropriate. Not every page needs a 7–10-item checklist or four panels.
3. **Prioritise outcomes over decoration.** Each interaction must help people identify, decide, practise, verify or respond. Avoid autoplay, looping motion, fake loading indicators and gimmicky scoring.
4. **Keep actual instructions concise and practical**, but never strip approved long-form content just to shorten a time label. On longer pages, improve scannability using informative headings, summaries and anchor links *only with editorial approval*.
5. **Use visuals sparingly and honestly.** Prefer properly licensed, real Nigerian workplace photography; if source/provenance is uncertain, label the asset as pending, not verified. Use clearly fictional screenshots for examples, never real customer data.
6. **Do not duplicate the BEC/Phishing format in Social Engineering.** It should emphasise behaviour, persuasion, false authority, urgency and how a staff member verifies a request through trusted channels across phone, WhatsApp or in person. The specific visual concept will be chosen during Social Engineering editorial review.
7. **Avoid repetitive labels.** Plain “What to do”, “Try this”, “Watch for”, “Before you share”, “When the call ends” can vary by purpose; clarity matters more than identical marketing language.

## Implementation review question

For every **new** page ask: “What one decision should a Nigerian SME owner or employee make more safely after reading this article, and does our chosen visual actually teach that decision?” If not, simplify or change the visual.


**Adoption:** This guidance applies to future, owner-approved content only. Existing pages, animation batches, copy, site structure, image placements and future Social Engineering drafting are not within this correction task.
