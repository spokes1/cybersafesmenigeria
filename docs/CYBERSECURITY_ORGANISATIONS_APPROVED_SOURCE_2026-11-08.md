# Who should your business contact when something goes wrong online?

A supplier says they have not received your payment. Your business phone suddenly loses service. A customer complains that their personal information has appeared somewhere it should not.

When something like this happens, knowing the right organisation to approach can save time and confusion.

Nigeria has agencies that handle cyber incidents, suspected crime, banking complaints, telecommunications issues and personal data protection. They do **different jobs**. Reporting to the wrong organisation, or waiting for a regulator when you could be securing an account, may delay the actions your business needs.

This directory explains where to start, when an issue may need to be escalated and which official website to use.

## Rule to remember

**ACT FIRST TO LIMIT HARM. THEN REPORT TO THE ORGANISATION THAT HANDLES THE PROBLEM.**

Reporting an incident does not automatically stop a fraudulent transfer, restore a stolen account or recover lost files.

## Start with the problem, not the agency name

### Money was sent to a suspicious account

**First:** Contact your bank or payment provider immediately through a verified fraud-support channel. Give the transaction reference and ask what urgent investigation, restriction or recall options may be available. Inform the business owner and preserve the transaction records.

**Then:** Consider reporting suspected cyber-enabled fraud to the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC). The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) may also be relevant to economic or financial crime. If your complaint concerns how a CBN-regulated institution handled the matter, use the bank's complaint process and escalate to the CBN where appropriate.

**Do not:** Wait for a police or regulatory report to be completed before notifying the bank. No agency can promise that transferred funds will be recovered.

### A business account is being misused

**First:** Use the account provider's official recovery process from a trusted device. Protect the business email, WhatsApp, social media or other affected account. Warn affected contacts through a verified channel where needed.

**Then:** Report an actual or suspected cyber incident to ngCERT. If the event appears to involve criminal unauthorised access, fraud or impersonation, consider the NPF-NCCC. A specialist may be needed to assess the affected systems.

### A phone number or SIM is not working as expected

**First:** Contact your mobile network operator through its official channels to check the line, secure it and investigate any suspected SIM replacement. Protect connected banking and messaging accounts.

**Then:** If the telecom complaint is unresolved, escalate to the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC), using the complaint reference from your operator. If fraud or identity misuse is suspected, an appropriate law-enforcement report may also be necessary.

### Customer or employee personal data may have been exposed

**First:** Stop further disclosure where practical, record what happened, establish what information and individuals may be affected, and seek appropriate assistance.

**Then:** Assess applicable obligations under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) is the relevant regulator. Certain personal data breaches require notification to the NDPC within 72 hours of awareness when the statutory risk threshold is met, with additional communication requirements for high-risk breaches. Not every lost file automatically meets those thresholds.

### A business computer may be infected

**First:** Stop sensitive work on the affected device and isolate it from the network where safe and appropriate. Preserve useful evidence and arrange trusted technical support.

**Then:** ngCERT provides cyber incident reporting and advisories. Where the incident involves suspected criminal activity, the NPF-NCCC may also be relevant.

**The first-response advice above is general.** When an incident affects several systems, large sums of money or sensitive records, seek qualified technical, financial or legal help promptly.

## The Nigerian organisations you should know

### Nigeria Computer Emergency Response Team (ngCERT)

**Best for:** Reporting cybersecurity incidents and finding Nigerian threat advisories.

ngCERT publishes information on emerging threats and provides channels for reporting incidents affecting organisations and individuals. It is an appropriate place to report suspicious cyber activity, malware or system compromises.

**Official website:** https://cert.gov.ng/

**Official incident reporting information:** https://cert.gov.ng/rfc2350

ngCERT's published profile lists a corporate incident-reporting route and explains what details to include. Its public social media accounts should not be treated as incident-reporting channels.

### Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC)

**Best for:** Suspected cybercrime, online fraud, impersonation, unauthorised access and digital evidence relevant to criminal investigation.

The NPF-NCCC provides an official electronic reporting route for suspected cybercrime. Be ready to give a factual account and relevant transaction or communication records.

**Official reporting portal:** https://nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport/

Only enter personal identification details through a verified official portal when necessary. Never give passwords, PINs or one-time codes to someone contacting you unexpectedly and claiming to be an investigator.

### Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)

**Best for:** Personal data protection requirements, privacy concerns and applicable breach notifications.

The NDPC oversees Nigeria's data protection framework. It is relevant when a business collects customer or employee information, receives a privacy complaint or experiences a personal data breach.

**Official website:** https://ndpc.gov.ng/

**Privacy breach reporting portal:** https://services.ndpc.gov.ng/breach/

Reporting duties depend on the nature of the incident and applicable legal conditions. Businesses should not treat this directory as a complete legal compliance guide.

### Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)

**Best for:** Escalating complaints about CBN-regulated financial institutions, including relevant banking and payment-service complaints.

The CBN publishes a consumer complaint procedure. Ordinarily, the business should first raise the complaint with its own regulated financial institution and retain the complaint reference. If the issue remains unresolved, follow the CBN's published escalation procedure.

**Official complaints guidance:** https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdcomgt.html

**Official website:** https://www.cbn.gov.ng/

**Important:** A suspected fraudulent transfer needs urgent action with the bank itself. The CBN complaints process is not a substitute for immediate fraud reporting.

### Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC)

**Best for:** Escalating unresolved complaints concerning telecommunications services, including mobile-number and SIM-related service issues.

Contact your network operator first, explain the problem and obtain a complaint ticket. Where the provider cannot resolve the issue satisfactorily, the NCC provides an official escalation process.

**Official consumer portal:** https://consumer.ncc.gov.ng/

**Official complaint form:** https://consumer.ncc.gov.ng/consumer-complaints/complaint-form

The NCC's published consumer guidance also lists the toll-free **622** line for escalated telecom complaints. In a suspected SIM-swap incident, do not wait for regulatory escalation before contacting the operator and securing connected accounts.

### Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC)

**Best for:** Reporting suspected economic and financial crimes through its official petition and reporting channels.

The EFCC investigates economic and financial crimes, including certain forms of fraud. Its site provides petition and reporting information. It can be relevant where a business has evidence of suspected financial crime.

**Official website:** https://www.efcc.gov.ng/

Do not assume that submitting a petition will reverse a payment. Notify your bank promptly and preserve your records.

### National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA)

**Best for:** Nigerian information-technology policy, standards, cybersecurity guidance and digital-skills resources.

NITDA develops and publishes IT-related guidelines and frameworks. Its cybersecurity and digital development work can help businesses learn about safer technology practices.

**Official website:** https://nitda.gov.ng/

**Standards and guidance:** https://nitda.gov.ng/regulations/

NITDA is **not presented here as the first point of contact for recovering a fraudulent bank transfer**.

## How to make a useful report

A clear report helps the receiving organisation understand what happened. You do not need to know the correct technical name for an attack before seeking help.

Prepare the information you have, without guessing:

1. **What happened:** A short, factual description of the suspicious event.
2. **When:** The date and approximate time you first noticed it.
3. **What was affected:** The business account, device, telephone line, transaction or type of information.
4. **What you have already done:** For example, contacted the bank, isolated a laptop or secured an account.
5. **Relevant evidence:** Transaction references, original messages, screenshots, complaint numbers or other appropriate records.
6. **How to reach you:** An authorised contact person for the business.

**Protect the evidence.** Do not post customers' private details publicly or send confidential records to unknown individuals claiming to be officials. Use the receiving organisation's verified reporting channel and provide only what it requires.

## Questions business owners often ask

### Should I contact ngCERT or the police?

They have different roles. ngCERT is a cybersecurity incident-response and advisory organisation. The NPF-NCCC handles suspected cybercrime investigations. Depending on the circumstances, a business may need to contact both, alongside an affected provider.

### If I have lost money, which organisation comes first?

Your **bank or payment provider**, immediately. Report suspected fraud and ask about the available urgent actions. Law-enforcement reporting may follow or happen alongside that, but it should not delay the bank notification.

### If my SIM has been swapped, should I complain to the NCC first?

Contact your mobile operator first to protect the number and obtain a complaint reference. The NCC generally handles unresolved service-provider complaints. Secure connected accounts and seek other help if criminal misuse is suspected.

### Do I need to know for certain that customer data was stolen before investigating?

No. Record what is known and assess potential exposure promptly. Whether and when notification to the NDPC is required depends on the applicable data-protection rules and risk assessment.

### Will reporting guarantee a refund, account recovery or prosecution?

No. Reporting starts or supports an appropriate process, but outcomes depend on the circumstances and the organisations involved.

**Save three verified contacts for your business today: your bank's fraud-support channel, your mobile provider's support channel and the official reporting page for suspected cybercrime.** Tell employees who should make each report and where transaction and account records are kept.

## Editorial safeguards

- Distinguish **first containment**, **criminal reporting**, **technical incident reporting**, **regulatory notification** and **complaint escalation**. These may overlap but are not interchangeable.
- Keep the NDPC 72-hour description conditional on the legal threshold; do not say every breach requires notification.
- Don't include unverified telephone numbers or unofficial short links. The NCC 622 line is specifically supported by its own consumer guidance.
- Don't claim reporting guarantees reimbursement or that an agency provides a free IT repair service.
- Avoid unsupported incident statistics, rankings, fabricated endorsements or real victim stories.
- CyberSafe itself does not collect incidents or customer records, and should not embed official agency forms into its pages.
- Keep CyberSafe's separate contact/domain work deferred.
- Preserve British English and avoid em dashes.
