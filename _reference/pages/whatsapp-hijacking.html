WhatsApp Hijacking

When your business chats and contacts fall into a stranger’s hands.

Check your protection Why it matters

If someone takes over your WhatsApp, your customers hear from them as you.

If someone takes over your WhatsApp account, they may impersonate you, contact your customers, and ask friends or business partners to send money. For an SME that uses WhatsApp to take orders, manage deliveries, or communicate with customers, the consequences can go beyond losing access to an app.

The golden rule

Never give anyone your WhatsApp verification code.

Never share the six-digit code
Turn on two-step verification

What is WhatsApp hijacking?

WhatsApp hijacking happens when someone gains unauthorised control of your account. One common method is tricking you into sharing the six-digit registration code WhatsApp sends when someone attempts to register your number.

With that code, a scammer may be able to register your number on another phone and take control of your account. They can then impersonate you, message your contacts, and make fraudulent requests in your name.

Scammer asks for your code
Registers your number on another phone
Takes control of your account
Messages contacts in your name

Why should your business care?

Your reputation is at risk

Customers may believe messages from a hijacked account are genuinely from you.

Customers and suppliers could lose money

A scammer may invent an emergency or request an urgent transfer.

Your business could lose an important communication channel

Orders, customer enquiries and delivery arrangements may be disrupted.

The scam can spread

An attacker may use a compromised account to trick other people into revealing their own verification codes.

You do not need to be famous to be targeted. If customers trust your WhatsApp number, the account has value to a scammer.

How does it happen?

A common approach begins with a message or phone call. Someone may claim that a verification code was sent to your number by mistake, or pretend to be WhatsApp support and ask you to read out a code.

Sometimes, the request comes from a friend or colleague whose own account has already been compromised. Because the message appears to come from someone you know, it may seem genuine.

“Sent to you by mistake”

Someone asks you to forward a code that was supposedly sent to your number by accident.

A fake support call

A caller pretends to be WhatsApp support and asks you to read out a code.

A friend whose account is compromised

The request looks genuine because it appears to come from someone you know.

Remember: A WhatsApp registration code is for registering your account. You should never share it with another person, regardless of who they claim to be.

WhatsApp’s two-step verification PIN is different from the six-digit registration code. Enabling two-step verification adds another layer of protection, but it does not make sharing your registration code safe.

Registration code

Six digits. Used to register your account. Never share it.

Two-step verification PIN

An extra layer of protection that only you should know.

Warning signs to watch for

Be cautious if you notice any of these signs:

A request to forward a code

Someone asks you to forward a WhatsApp verification code, supposedly sent by mistake.

Pressure to hurry

A message creates urgency, such as “send it quickly before it expires.”

A fake support caller

A caller claims to be WhatsApp support and asks you to reveal a code.

A familiar contact asks for money

A familiar contact suddenly asks for money or sends unusual messages.

Unexpected logout

WhatsApp unexpectedly logs you out, or tells you that your number is no longer registered on your phone.

An unfamiliar linked device

You notice an unfamiliar device under Settings → Linked Devices.

An unexpected logout does not prove that your account has been hijacked, but you should investigate it promptly.

What should you do?

If someone asks for your code

  1. Do not share or forward the code.
  2. Do not click suspicious links or follow instructions from an unverified caller.
  3. Contact the person through a separate channel, such as a normal phone call, if you need to verify their request.

If you suspect your account has been hijacked

  1. Try to register your number again.Open the official WhatsApp app on your phone and follow the registration process using your number and the code sent to you. Successfully registering your number again will generally log out the other phone. If you cannot regain access, use WhatsApp’s official Help Centre.
  2. Follow the two-step verification recovery process if needed.If you are asked for a PIN you did not set or cannot remember, follow WhatsApp’s official recovery instructions. Do not assume the PIN and registration code are the same.
  3. Check Linked Devices.Once you have regained access, open Settings → Linked Devices and log out any device you do not recognise.
  4. Warn your contacts immediately.Use calls, SMS or another channel to tell customers, friends and suppliers that your account may have been compromised. Ask them to ignore unusual payment requests or requests for verification codes.
  5. Secure related accounts.If you suspect that your email, phone or another account has also been compromised, secure it from a trusted device.
  6. Report financial losses.Contact your bank promptly if money has been transferred fraudulently. You can also report the incident to the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre at nccc.npf.gov.ng/ereport.
Avoid sharing verification codes with anyone claiming they can recover your account for you.

A Nigerian example

Reported by TheCable.

Former Senator Tokunbo Afikuyomi described how fraudsters took control of his WhatsApp account and sent money requests to his contacts.

Account taken over
Money requests sent to contacts
Some call to confirm and avoid the scam

Some people called him directly to confirm the requests and avoided the scam, while others reportedly sent money to accounts supplied by the fraudsters.

The lesson: a message appearing to come from someone you know is not enough to prove that the request is genuine. A direct phone call outside WhatsApp can help you confirm an unusual request before sending money.

This risk is not limited to public figures.
